### 在实际承诺做一个AI项目之前，吴恩达通常会自己或让工程师做"技术尽职调查"——仔细看数据、看A和B的输入输出，认真判断这件事AI到底能不能做；问题在于，有些CEO对AI期望过高，会让工程师去做今天AI根本做不到的事情；而媒体和学术文献又只报道成功故事，我们只看到一串成功案例，看不到失败案例，导致有些人以为AI什么都能做——这当然不是真的

### 一个不太完美但很实用的经验法则：几乎所有你花不到一秒钟思考就能做的事情，现在或不久的将来，大概率可以用监督学习的A到B映射来自动化——比如判断其他车辆的位置是一秒内能做的事，看手机有没有划痕是一秒内能做的事，听懂别人说了什么也就几秒钟的思考量；这个法则不完美，但能帮你快速筛选出AI系统可能胜任的任务

> 之前你看到了AI应用的那个列表——从垃圾邮件过滤到语音识别到机器翻译等等。有一个不太完美但很实用的经验法则，可以用来判断监督学习大概能做什么、不能做什么：几乎所有你花不到一秒钟思考就能做的事，现在或不久的将来，大概都可以用监督学习——也就是输入到输出的映射——来自动化。所以，判断其他车辆的位置，是你不到一秒就能做的事。判断手机有没有划痕，看一眼就能判断，用不了一秒。理解——至少是转录——别人说了什么，也用不了几秒钟思考。虽然这是个不完美的经验法则，但它也许能帮你快速想出一些AI系统能做哪些任务的例子。

> Previously, you saw this list of AI applications, from spam protein to speech recognition to machine translation, and so on. One imperfect rule of thumb you can use to decide what supervised learning may or may not be able to do is that pretty much anything you could do with a second of thought, you can probably now or soon automate using supervised learning, using this input-output mapping. So for example, in order to determine the position of other cars, that's something that you can do with less than a second. In order to tell if a phone is scratched, you can look at it and you can kind of tell in less than a second. In order to understand, at least transcribe what was said, you know, doesn't take that many seconds of thought. And while this is an imperfect rule of thumb, it maybe gives you a way to quickly think of some examples of tasks that AI systems can do.

### 反之，预测股市就是今天AI做不到的事——仅凭一只股票的历史价格（A）去预测未来价格（B），这是人类一秒钟做不到、甚至花更长时间也做不到的事，机器学习也很难做到；如果你硬要用机器学习，简单算法可能拟合出一条直线——但根据你选的时间窗口不同，红色线和蓝色线给出的结果会天差地别；根本问题是：股票的历史价格对未来价格的预测能力非常低，AI无法准确预测如此随机的东西

> 相比之下，今天AI做不到的事情，一个例子就是预测股市——比如仅凭一只股票的历史价格去预测未来的价格。这是一件人类大概不可能一秒钟做到、甚至超过一秒钟也做不到的事，而且大概也不可能让机器学习准确地做到。我们来仔细看看。假设我们要处理的任务是：给定一只股票近期的价格——这是输入A——预测未来某个时间点、比如一个月后的价格——这是输出B。如果你对这个问题应用机器学习会发生什么？一个简单的算法可能会尝试拟合一条直线穿过数据，但取决于你选择哪个时间段去拟合，你可能会得到这样的红色线，或者如果你选择这个窄窗口拟合，可能会得到这条蓝色线。所以根据实现细节的不同，这个算法可能会给出天差地别的输出值。这个应用的主要问题是：股票过去的价格历史对未来股价的预测能力非常低——这就是为什么用机器学习做这件事的尝试一直没有成功。未来股价的随机性太强了，AI很难准确预测。顺便说一句，为了完整性我需要补充：仅凭同一只股票的历史价格来预测股价似乎是不可能做到的。但确实有些股票交易者有时候会找到其他输入——比如，如果他们合法获取了某些网络流量或客流量数据，帮他们估算出公司销售额，那么这些数据结合历史价格数据，也许能让算法有一些预测能力。然而，这些额外的输入通常很复杂或成本很高，而且仍然无法克服股市本身固有的随机性。

> Whereas in contrast, something that AI today cannot do would be actually predicting the stock market. Say, predict the future price of some company stock given only the historical price of that stock. It's a thing that a person probably can't do in one second or even longer than a second and it's probably not possible to get machine learning to do this accurately either. Let's look in greater detail at this. Say the task we want to tackle is, given the recent price of a stock, that's the input A, predict the price at a future point in time, say a month into the future, that's the output B. What would happen if you were to apply machine learning to this? Well, a simpler algorithm might try to fit a straight line to the data, but depending on what period of time you fit the data to, you might get a red line like this, or if you fit it to this narrow window, you might get this blue line. So depending on the details of the implementation, this particular algorithm might give wildly varying output values. The main problem with this application is that the past history of a stock price is just not very predictive of the future stock price, which is why attempts to use machine learning this way haven't been successful. Future stock prices are so random that it's just hard for AI to predict it accurately. By the way, for completeness, I should say that predicting stock price based only on the historical price of the same stock seems to be impossible, but there are stock traders that sometimes find other inputs. For example, if they manage to legally obtain some web traffic or foot traffic data that helps them estimate what the company's sales were, then that, in combination with the historical price data, might make it possible for the algorithm to have some predictive power. However, these other inputs are typically complex or costly to acquire and still can't overcome the intrinsically somewhat random nature of the stock market.

### 帮你在筛选项目时快速判断可行性的另外两条经验法则：第一，学习"简单概念"更可能成功——虽然没有正式定义，但如果一件事你只需要不到一秒或几秒钟就能得出结论，那就可以认为是个简单概念，比如自动驾驶里识别前方车辆是个相对简单的概念，而想出一个聪明信号去预测某公司销售额就不是；第二，有大量数据（包括输入A和输出B都有的数据）的项目更可能成功——比如判断手机有没有划痕，如果你有几千张手机照片同时标注了A（图片）和B（有没有划痕），那做出一个高准确率的检测系统就大概率可行

> 为了进一步帮你磨练快速筛选可行/不可行项目的直觉，这里还有另外两条经验法则，关于什么让一个机器学习问题更容易或更可能可行。第一：学习简单概念更可能可行。但什么叫"简单概念"？这没有正式定义，但如果一件事你只需要花不到一秒钟、或者很少几秒钟的脑力思考就能得出结论，那这就暗示它可能是个简单概念。所以，如果你从自动驾驶车辆的窗外往外看，去识别其他车——这看起来是个相对简单的概念。相比之下，想出一个聪明信号去预测某公司的销售额——这看起来就不那么像一个简单概念。第二：如果你有大量可用数据，机器学习问题就更可能可行。这里说的数据既包括输入A也包括输出B——也就是你在A到B、输入到输出映射中需要AI系统具备的两样东西。所以，举个例子，判断手机有没有划痕这个任务中，输入A是一组手机图片，输出B是标注手机是否被划伤的标签。如果你有几千张手机图片，同时有A和B，那你做出一个高准确率划痕检测机器学习系统的概率就会高很多。

> To hold your intuition about how to quickly filter feasible or not feasible projects, here are a couple of other rules of thumb about what makes a machine learning problem easier or more likely to be feasible. One, learning a simple concept is more likely to be feasible, but what does it mean for something to be a simple concept? There's no formal definition of it, but if it is something that takes you less than a second of mental thought, or maybe just a very small number of seconds of mental thought to come up with a conclusion, then that would be suggestive of it being a simple concept. So, if you're looking outside the window of a self-driving car to spot the other cars, well, that would seem like a relatively simple concept. Whereas in contrast, trying to come up with clever signals to predict a given company's sales, well, that seems like less of a simple concept. Second, a machine learning problem is more likely to be feasible if you have lots of data available. Here, data means both the input A and the output B that you want the AI system to have in your A to B or input output mapping. So for example, in trying to determine whether a phone is scratched or not, the input A would be a set of images of phones, and the output B could be a label identifying the phone as being scratched or not scratched. Then if you have thousands of pictures of phones with both A and B, the odds of you building a machine learning system to detect scratches accurately would be much higher.
