## 一、AI转型指南第1部分——帮助一家优秀公司变成AI优秀公司需要分五步：执行试点项目获取动能、建立内部AI团队、提供广泛的AI培训、制定AI战略、做内外沟通，前三个步骤是打基础的关键

### 第一步"执行试点项目获取动能"的核心原则是：初期项目成功最重要，不一定选最有价值的项目——吴恩达在领导Google Brain团队时，第一个内部客户选的是语音识别团队（语音识别对公司来说"锦上添花"但远不如搜索和广告有价值），正是因为成功才吸引了更多团队合作，第二个客户是Google Maps，成功后才有机会接触广告团队；选一个能在6到12个月内展现出效果的项目，前一两项目可以外包给外部专家以更快获得动能

> 怎么帮你的公司变得擅长AI？基于吴恩达领导Google Brain团队和百度AI部门的经验，他写了一本"AI转型指南"。这套指南包含五个步骤：第一步，执行试点项目获取动能，开始感受做AI项目是什么感觉；第二步，建立内部AI团队；第三步，提供广泛AI培训，不仅给工程师，还要覆盖公司各层级包括高管；第四步，制定AI战略；第五步，做内部和外部沟通。这五个步骤不一定要严格按顺序，可能会有重叠，但这个编号大致反映了建议的顺序。先说第一步：执行试点项目。如果你想让公司获得AI动能，最重要的考虑是初期项目要成功，而不一定是最有价值的项目。吴恩达当时在Google Brain时，深度学习还受到很多怀疑，他第一个内部客户是Google语音识别团队——语音识别有用但并不是公司最重要的项目，对公司的直接价值远远不如搜索和在线广告。但正是因为他的团队让语音识别团队变得更成功，才开始收到了正面反馈，其他团队开始对AI感兴趣了。第二个内部客户是Google Maps，用深度学习提高地图数据质量。有了前两次成功，他才开始跟广告团队谈。所以选初期项目时，选你觉得成功概率高的，哪怕它不是最有价值的。最好能在6到12个月内展现出成果，快速获得动能。

> How can you help your company become good at AI? Based on my experience starting leading the Google Brain team as well as Baidu's AI group, which were respectively the leading forces for helping Google and Baidu become good at deep learning AI, I've spent a lot of time thinking about what it takes to help a great company become a great AI company. And I wound up writing an AI transformation playbook to help other companies on this journey. In this video, I'd like to share with you the details of the AI transformation playbook so that you can better understand what it might take for your company to become good at AI. In case some of this seems like things that only CEOs need to know, I think that's not the case. It's useful for everyone in the company to understand what it might take for your work to impact not just a few projects, but maybe have a bigger impact on the company as a whole. Let's get started. Share the five steps that the AI transformation playbook will dive into greater detail in a little bit. But briefly, step one is for your company to execute pilot projects to gain momentum. Start to know what it feels like to work on AI projects. Step two is to build an in-house AI team. Step three is to provide broad AI training, not just to engineers, but to many levels within the company, including executives. Step four is to develop your AI strategy. And step five is to develop internal and external communications about your company and AI. The way your company will execute the steps may not be totally sequential, and so the different steps may overlap, but this numbering gives a maybe rough sense of the order in which I think you could do these steps. In this video, we will go in greater depth on the first three of these three steps, and the next video will cover steps four and five. Let's start with step one, executing pilot projects to gain momentum. If you want your company to gain momentum with AI, the most important consideration for the initial project or projects is for them to be successful, rather than necessarily be the most valuable. For example, when I was leading the Google Brain team, there was still a lot of skepticism at that time about deep learning. So my first internal customer was Google's speech recognition team. And speech recognition is nice to have. It's useful, but it's actually not the most important or valuable project for the company's bottom lines. It's not as valuable as, for example, web search or online advertising. But by having my team make the Google speech team more successful, it started to fly real turning, help us get momentum, because then the peers, the other teams that are brothers and sisters, the speech team, started to see my team make the speech team more successful. And so they also started to gain favor in AI and wanted to work with us. So my second internal customer was the Google Maps team to improving the quality of the maps data using deep learning. And with the first and second successes, I then started other conversations with the online advertising team, for example. So when selecting your initial project, try to pick something that you think has a good chance of success that can start to fly real turning, even if it may not be the most valuable project that you could eventually do for the company. Because the goal of the first few projects is just to gain momentum is also nice. If you can pick something that can show traction within six to 12 months, so you can start to fly with returning quickly. Finally, your first one or two pilot projects can be either in-house or outsourced. If you do not yet have a large in-house AI team, it might be possible, might even be advisable to outsource some or all of your first couple AI projects in order to get more expertise in-house and to let you start building that momentum faster.

### 第二步"建立内部AI团队"的推荐架构是：集中式AI团队+矩阵式人才安排——建立一个中央AI部门，把AI人才以矩阵方式分配到各业务单元（BU），比如礼品卡BU的负责人可能很懂礼品卡但不懂AI，难以自己招聘和管理AI人才；中央AI团队的好处包括：统一招聘标准、给AI工程师提供交流社区、构建面向全公司的平台和基础设施；这个AI部门可以放在CTO/CIO/首席数据官/首席数字官下，也可以新设"首席AI官"（CAIO）——这个职位吴恩达看到越来越多公司在用；同时建议由CEO提供初始资金启动AI部门，而不是让AI部门从各BU拉经费

> 过了一定阶段你就需要自己的内部AI团队来执行长期系列AI项目了。第二步是建立内部AI团队。很多公司是CEO下面多个业务单元（BU）这样组织。我推荐大多数公司先建一个集中式AI团队，然后以矩阵组织方式把人才分配到各BU支持他们工作。为什么要集中式？举个例子：假设有一个礼品卡BU，其负责人可能很擅长礼品卡业务，但如果不了解AI、不知道如何招聘、留住和管理AI人才，让这个BU负责人自己去弄AI团队就很难。不如找一个AI团队负责人来负责全公司统一的招聘标准、人才保留，并让AI工程师有个社区可以互相交流AI怎么应用到各自的业务领域。然后把这些AI人才矩阵分配到礼品卡BU，跟礼品卡领域专家一起开发AI项目。此外中央AI团队还可以建公司级平台——如果某个软件平台或数据基础设施全公司都能用，单个BU可能没有资源和动力去建，但中央AI团队可以。这个AI部门可以放在CTO/CIO/首席数据官/首席数字官下面，也可以设一个"首席AI官"（CAIO），吴恩达看到这个职位越来越普及。最后一个小建议：最好由CEO直接拨款来启动AI部门，而不是让AI部门一开始就去各BU拉经费，初始投资之后AI部门需要向BU证明自己创造的价值，但CEO前期注入资金能让AI部门更快获得动能。

> Now, beyond a certain point, you will need your own in-house AI team to execute a long-term sequence of maybe many dozens of AI projects. And so step two is to build an in-house AI team. A lot of companies are organized like this, where there's a CEO and multiple business units, which I'm going to abbreviate with BU, that report up to the CEO. So what I recommend for most companies is to build a centralized AI team and then to take the talent in the matrix organization and to matrix them into these different business units to support their work. Why a centralized AI team? Let's take an example. Maybe this unit is your gift card business unit and the BU leader may be great at whatever he or she does. They may be great at the gift card business. But unless he or she is knowledgeable AI and knows how to build, retain and manage an AI team, it may be very difficult for that business unit leader to hire and retain and appropriately manage their own AI talent. So in that case, I think you're also successfully much higher if you find an AI team leader that can be responsible for consistent, company-wide standards for recruiting, retention, have a centralized AI team to give the team a community to talk to each other about how AI applies to your business vertical. It may be more efficient to take the AI talent in your centralized AI unit and matrix them into the gift card business unit so that your AI talent can work together with the gift card domain experts in order to develop interesting AI projects together. One other responsibility for the AI unit is to build company-wide platforms. If there are software platforms or other tools or data infrastructure that could be useful for the whole company, then a single business unit may not have the resources or the incentive to build these company-wide platforms or resources that can support the whole company. But the centralized AI team may be able to help build these company-wide tools or platforms that can help multiple business units. Finally, this new AI business unit can be under the CTO, the CIO, the chief data officer or the chief digital officer, or it could also be under a new chief AI officer. The CAIO chief AI officer is a role that I'm seeing more and more often in different companies, but if some other senior executive has the right skillset, they could also manage the AI unit. Finally, one last recommendation, which is that I think it is helpful if to get the AI unit started, the company or the CEO provides funding to build up the AI unit rather than require the AI unit to get funding from the business units. Eventually, after the initial investment and after the initial ramp up, the AI unit will have to show us value that is creating for the business units. But having CEO inject funding at the outset so that we can get going will often help you get that initial momentum much faster.

### 第三步"提供广泛AI培训"按层分级：高管和高级业务领导约需4小时培训，学AI能为企业做什么、AI战略基础知识、做资源分配决策；负责AI项目的部门领导约需至少12小时培训，要学如何设定项目方向、做技术和商业尽职调查、做部门级资源分配、跟踪AI项目进度；软件工程师要成为AI专业人才约需至少100小时培训——学构建和交付AI软件、采集管理数据、在具体AI项目上高效执行；世界严重缺乏AI工程师，所以内部培训是很多公司建立AI能力的关键手段；培训方式建议通过在线课程、书籍、YouTube视频、博客等已有优质内容，由COO组织专家来"策展"这些内容并激励团队完成学习，而不是自创内容

> 除了建立内部团队，我还要推荐广泛AI培训。要让公司擅长AI，不是只有工程师需要懂AI，公司各层级的人都需要理解AI怎么跟自己的角色交互。比如高管和高级业务领导需要学AI能为企业做什么、AI战略基础知识、以及足够做资源分配决策的AI知识——这类培训大约4小时就够。负责AI项目的部门领导要学怎么设定项目方向、怎么做技术和商业尽职调查、怎么在部门层面做资源分配决策、怎么跟踪AI项目进度——这类培训至少需要12小时。最后，很多公司在外面招AI人才，但也不要低估把现有工程师培养成AI人才的巨大影响。一个软件工程师要变成AI专业人才需要一段时间，至少规划100小时的培训。世界今天严重缺乏AI工程师，所以内部培训是很多公司建立AI能力的关键途径。培训怎么做呢？现在有大量优质在线内容——在线课程、书籍、YouTube视频、博客文章——一个好的COO应该跟专家合作，去"策展"这些内容并激励团队完成学习，而不是自己去创作内容，因为那要贵得多。

> In addition to building an in-house AI team, I also recommend that you provide broad AI training. Now, for a company to become good at AI, it's not just that you need engineers to know AI, you need multiple people at multiple levels of the company to understand how AI interacts with their roles. For example, for executives and senior business leaders, I recommend that they learn what AI can do for your enterprise, that they learn the basics at least of AI strategy, and they learn enough about AI to make resource allocation decisions. So how much training should executives and senior business leaders receive? I think that numbers of hours of training is not a very good way to measure training, but with that caveat, I think you can deliver a lot of this training with maybe four hours or so of training. Leaders of divisions work on AI projects also need to know how to interact in their role with AI. I think these leaders would need to understand how to set project direction, so how to conduct technical and business diligence, how to make resource allocation decisions at the division level, as well as how to track and monitor progress of AI projects. So this type of training, I think, would take at least 12 hours, although again, number of hours is not a great metric for tracking how much you're learning. Finally, many companies are hiring AI talent from outside, but I would also not underestimate the importance and the impact of training up your existing engineering workforce with AI skills. For a software engineer to become professional at AI does take a while, so plan for maybe at least 100 hours of training. But I'm seeing many companies provide training to help engineers learn to build and ship AI software to gather and manage data, as well as help them become effective at executing on specific AI projects. The world today does not have nearly enough AI engineers, and so in-house training is a key part of many companies building up of their in-house AI capabilities. Finally, how do you get all this training done? Since the rise of online digital content, ranging from, of course, online courses to also books and YouTube videos and blog posts, there is a lot of great content online about all of these subjects, and I think a good COO should work of experts to curate this type of content and motivate the teams to complete these learning activities rather than necessarily create content, which is much more expensive thing to do.
